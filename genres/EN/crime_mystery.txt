1. Write the COMPLETE chapter. Target ~3,200 words. Focus on tension, pacing, and detailed observation.
2. Strict third-person limited, past tense. Locked to the detective/investigator POV. No head-hopping.
3. Hit all outline beats. Focus on observation, deduction, and interviews.
4. Sensory precision: describe spaces, lighting, layout, and exact physical clues (ashes, thread, scrap of paper).
5. Dialogue is a verbal chess match: characters lie, evade, omit, or reveal things under psychological pressure.
6. Pay close attention to physical micro-expressions of guilt or evasion (e.g. rapid pulse, tightening collar, delayed answers).
7. High suspense: slow the pacing down during search scenes or dark encounters.
8. Metaphors are grounded in shadows, decay, machinery, observation, or cold realism.
9. Sentences vary: long, clause-heavy sentences during slow detective analysis; short, fast sentences during action/revelation.
10. Trust the reader. Never explain the clue's significance immediately. Let the reader piece it together.

PATTERNS TO AVOID:
11. NO easy resolutions. The protagonist cannot magically guess secrets; they must observe, ask, and deduce.
12. NO flowery, overly romantic, or dramatic narration. Keep the narrator's voice clinical, observant, and sharp.
13. NO vague descriptions. If there is a sound, describe its exact timbre and direction. If there is a smell, pinpoint it.
14. NO section breaks as rhythm crutches -- max 1 per chapter to show physical movement to a new crime scene.
15. NO dialogue tags that betray the mystery (e.g. "he lied nervously"). Let the physical action show the lie.
16. END the chapter on a lingering question, a new piece of evidence, or a cold, quiet threat.
