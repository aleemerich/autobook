World.MD – Cosmology & History:

- Foundation myth: tropeiros found a spring of crystal water from a stone shaped like an open book; they swore to build a village where registration of life was sacred.
- Name “São Brás” from saint protector of throats; “das Almas” added by residents believing each name recorded in leather becomes part of the soul.
- First registration and first lie: In 1852, tropeiro Joaquim Silveira registered birth of his son as “Joaquim Filho”, although the child was stillborn. Lie discovered three days later when midwife washed the body and saw child not breathing. Cartório did not punish Joaquim, but the book got a dark stain that never left.
- Era of Coffee and growth (1880-1930): With opening of Estrada de Ferro Centro‑Oeste, city exported coffee from southern valleys. Income increase brought new justice officials, primary school, first local press. Cartório remained only place to register birth, marriage, death; other bureaucracy depended on it. Custom of calling each person by full name at first meeting to confirm registration.
- Great Drought and first wave of “sem nome” (1942): Three-year drought caused families to leave farms for city seeking work. Stress led to lies about age, marital status, existence of absent relatives. In 1945, first documented case of “sem nome”: seamstress Dona Lúcia, who had declared herself widow to get pension, lost the “L” of her name and with it memory of husband who taught her to sew. Treated as local curiosity; nobody understood mechanism.
- Era of television and growing silence (1970-1990): TV arrival brought images of big centers, aspiration to change life. Youths falsified documents to get jobs in Belo Horizonte factories. Number of lies increased, but cartorio still had no way to detect them besides “sem nome” symptom. In 1983, priest of matrix began preaching that “truth is the only water that does not fill the well of forgetting”. Sermon recorded on cassette and circulated among elders, but didn't stop phenomenon growth.
- Current surge (2018-present): In last six years, number of “sem nome” jumped from less than ten to about sixty. Lies now involve omission of crimes, falsification of death acts, denial of own ethnic origin. Cartório, now under Dona Celina, is only place to compare what was written with what is remembered. Present tension comes from imbalance: institution that should preserve memory becoming mirror of mass forgetting.

Now Core Systems, Rules & Constraints:

Hard / Operational Rules:

1. Registro como âncora da alma:
   - Every birth, marriage, death must be handwritten, black ink, in leather books of cartório.
   - Full name (first name + surname(s)) must be written exactly as used in daily life.
   - Cost: scrivener must spend, on average, fifteen minutes per registration, including identity verification with witnesses.

2. Detecção de mentira pessoal:
   - When a person states, aloud or in writing, something that contradicts what is recorded in their own book (e.g., claim married when record shows single, or deny existence of a father that appears in birth book), cartório “knows”.
   - Effect not immediate; person wakes next day and, when called by someone, either cannot pronounce own name, or is called by incorrect name.
   - Limitation: detection only works for lies about own past. Lies about others' facts (e.g., saying neighbor stole chickens) do not trigger effect.

3. Progressão do esquecimento:
   - Each letter that disappears from name in collective memory erases a specific affective memory, following fixed order:
       1st letter → who gave the name (usually father or mother who chose it).
       2nd letter → first romantic kiss.
       3rd letter → face of most loved person (partner, child, close friend).
       4th letter → memory of first word spoken.
       5th letter → feeling of belonging to childhood home.
   - After fifth letter, process continues erasing affective memories in reverse chronological order until only factual memory remains (dates, street names, professional skills).
   - Cost: subject loses ability to feel emotion linked to erased memories, but retains intellect and motor skills.

4. Irreversibilidade:
   - Once lost, letter cannot be recovered by common means (rewriting name, repeating name aloud, therapy).
   - Only way to stop advancement is to undo original lie: remove false record or add truth to book, accompanied by public declaration of remorse before two cartório witnesses.
   - Limitation: confession must be written, with signature of liar and two witnesses who are not first-degree relatives.

5. Transparência social:
   - People who lost at least one letter of name are perceived as “ligeiramente transparentes”: their vision seems slightly blurred when looking directly at someone; their voices seem muffled, as if coming from adjacent room.
   - Effect is subjective: those who know person well do not notice change, but strangers describe as “seems to be going out of focus in an old photograph”.
   - Cost: “sem nome” often ignored in lines, does not receive formal greetings, excluded from informal invitations, although continues to work normally.

Exceptions, Edge Cases, and Unusual Abilities:

- Mentira compulsiva vs. mentira pontual:
   - A lie told under extreme coercion (e.g., death threat) still triggers effect, but progression slower: first letter disappears only after three days instead of one. Suggests cartório considers degree of voluntariness.

- Registro coletivo:
   - Marriages and deaths involve two parties; if only one lies about own past (e.g., groom claims single when divorced), effect hits only the liar. Partner not affected unless also lied.

- Uso de apelidos:
   - Informal nicknames (e.g., “Zezinho”, “Nha Benta”) not protected by system. Person can lose official name and still be called by nickname, as nickname not in leather books. However, if nickname used officially in some document (e.g., work card), it falls under same rule.

- Escrivães e a “leitura silenciosa”:
   - Scrivener working in cartório develops, after five years of service, heightened perception of inconsistencies in books: passing finger over page feels slight resistance when there is a lie recorded. Ability not infallible (~70% accuracy) and causes mental fatigue; after two hours continuous reading, scrivener starts confusing own memories with those in books.

- Objetos de papel antigo:
   - Books, letters, documents written with iron-gall ink and kept >50 years seem to “absorb” part of cartório's energy. When such object read aloud near cartório, letters of reader's name may temporarily glow (visible only to those with keen perception). Effect lasts max ten minutes and disappears if object removed from cartório room.

Geography section:

Localização e relevo:
- São Brás das Almas situated at 980 meters altitude, in Serra do Espinhaço, between coordinates 19°12′S and 43°45′W.
- Access via state road MG‑126, which winds through narrow valleys and crosses three stone bridges built in 1930s.
- City occupies a half‑moon shaped valley, surrounded by hills covered with altitude fields and fragments of Atlantic forest.

Clima:
- Winter dry and cold: minimum temps 2°C, maximum 18°C; frequent light frosts in lower valleys.
- Summer humid and mild: minimums 14°C, maximums 26°C; rains concentrated Nov-Mar, with strong downpours leaving smell of wet earth and mold of old paper on streets.
- Annual average relative humidity 78%; morning fog common Apr-Aug, sticking to stone walls and leaving greenish gleam on walls.

Arquitetura e materiais:
- Houses built of rough stone extracted from own slopes, walls thick up to 60 cm maintaining stable internal temperature.
- Roofs of red ceramic tile, overlaid with clay layer for waterproofing.
- Doors of jequitibá wood, often carved with family names on inner side — custom started when cartório didn't exist and families engraved names on doors to avoid losing them.
- Streets of irregular stone, average width 2.8 m, insufficient for two vehicles to pass simultaneously. Steps echo as light “tic‑tic” due to contrast between hard stone and serra dust accumulating in crevices.

Infraestrutura:
- Water supply: two springs captured in concrete boxes on northern serra; water pumped by gravity to 150 m² reservoir on Alto do Morro do Cruzeiro, then distributed via cast iron pipes.
- Electricity: grid linked to Capão Branco hydro plant, 32 km away; power outages rare, but when occur city relies on diesel generators in pousadas and matrix.
- Sewage: septic tank per lot; no collector network. Smell of damp earth mixes with light sewage odor during heavy rain.
- Communication: a Claro cellular tower installed on Alto do Morro do Telefone; signal weak in deep valleys, leading many to still use landline phones in houses.

Pontos de referência sensoriais:
- Praça Matriz: irregular stone pavement, shaded by three yellow ipês that bloom Jul-Sep; wind carries flower scent mixed with copal incense used in masses.
- Mercado Municipal: covered by fibro‑cement tile; air dense with smell of minas cheese, smoked linguiça, and potato soil coming directly from farms.
- Cartório: two‑story building, smooth stone façade; dominant smell of aged paper, leather cured with tannin from mimosa bark, and light linseed oil used to bind books. Inside, silence so dense you can hear own heartbeat.
- Cachoeira da Fumaça: 15 m drop hidden behind quartzite wall; mist formed at pool smells of wet stone and leaves skin slightly tingling, as if touched by static electricity.
